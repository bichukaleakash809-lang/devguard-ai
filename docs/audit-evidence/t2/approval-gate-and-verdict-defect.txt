============================================================================
DEFECT 1 — THE HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP APPROVAL GATE NEVER OPENED
============================================================================

README: '**Human-in-the-loop approval gate** for critical/high severity fixes'

The gate decision was:
    severity = str(getattr(req, "severity", "")).lower()
    gated = severity in ("critical", "high")

  ScanRequest.model_fields = ['code', 'language']
  ScanRequest(code=..., severity='critical') -> getattr(r,'severity','') == ''

  There is no `severity` field. Pydantic drops the extra key, so getattr
  returned '' EVERY time and `gated` was permanently False.

  Consequence: every scan auto-finalized, critical ones included.
  POST /scan/{id}/approve and /reject were unreachable dead code, and the
  'pending_approval' status was never emitted. The safety control that
  stops an unreviewed critical security fix from being applied did not run.

  The design was wrong twice over: even with the field present, the REQUEST
  cannot know the severity — nothing has scanned the code when it arrives.

AFTER — the gate reads the Scanner's finding:

  findings                                severity              status
  one low                                      low           completed
  one medium                                medium           completed
  one high                                    high    pending_approval
  one critical                            critical    pending_approval
  low + critical + medium                 critical    pending_approval
  nothing found                               None           completed

  the worst finding decides, not the first — and nothing found means
  nothing to approve.

  client sends severity='low' with a CRITICAL finding -> pending_approval
  -> a caller cannot talk the gate out of opening. Reading the scan closes
     a bypass that reading the request left open.

DEFECT 2 — THE AUDIT TRAIL RECORDED 'unknown' FOR EVERY SCAN
============================================================================

SECURITY.md: 'Tamper-evident audit trail. Every scan appends a hash-chained
record.' True — but the record's one substantive field was never written:

    verdict=str(getattr(result, "verdict", "unknown"))

  'verdict' in PipelineResult.model_fields: False
  The verdict lives at final_validation.verdict, so the default won.

MEASURED against the log committed in this repository:
    entries: 35
      verdict='unknown': 35

  35 of 35. A cryptographically sound, tamper-evident chain of records
  that say nothing. Those entries are NOT rewritten — they honestly record
  what the system knew, and editing a hash-chained audit log to look better
  is precisely the act the chain exists to detect.

AFTER — four distinguishable outcomes:

  Validator passed           -> verdict='pass'
  Validator failed           -> verdict='fail'
  Scanner found nothing      -> verdict='no_findings'
  findings, no validation    -> verdict='unvalidated'

  'unknown' now means one thing only: the read failed. It is no longer
  indistinguishable from 'the Scanner found nothing'.

  chain across mixed verdicts: valid=True, entries=4
